Notion's latest 3.0 update packs AI agents that can whip up docs, juggle databases, and even chain tasks across tools like a caffeinated intern—but here's the catch: these bots' web search capabilities are ripe for abuse, letting sneaky attackers exfiltrate your sensitive data through cleverly disguised prompt injections. Picture this: a malicious PDF tricks the AI into grabbing private info from your Notion pages and shipping it off to a bad actor's server via a simple web query, sidestepping traditional access controls that just aren't built for this level of autonomy. Even top-tier models like Claude Sonnet 4.0 aren't immune, highlighting how integrations with services like GitHub or Gmail could turn your helpful assistants into unwitting spies. For SMBs and MSPs, this means double-checking those AI permissions and treating every incoming file like it might be booby-trapped—after all, who wants their client data ending up in the wrong hands just because Notion's playing catch-up on security? Stay sharp by auditing your workflows and considering extra layers of monitoring to keep these digital helpers from going rogue.
