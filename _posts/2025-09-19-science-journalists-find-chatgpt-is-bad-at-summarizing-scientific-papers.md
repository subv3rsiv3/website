Summary Science journalists from AAAS recently tested ChatGPT's ability to summarize complex scientific papers, and the results were underwhelming—scores averaged below 2.5 out of 5 for accuracy and appeal. The AI excelled at basic transcription of straightforward content but faltered badly with nuances, like confusing correlation with causation or ignoring key contexts and limitations. This highlights AI's unreliability for in-depth scientific tasks, which could lead SMBs and MSPs astray if they use tools like ChatGPT for research or content generation without oversight. For tech-savvy small businesses, this means potential pitfalls in decision-making, such as basing product development on flawed summaries that overstate findings. Ultimately, while AI might improve with updates like GPT-5, it's not yet a trustworthy shortcut for handling scientific material. Takeaways: - Double-check AI summaries to catch factual distortions. - Factor in extra editing time for AI outputs, increasing costs.

        **Key takeaways**
        - Avoid using AI for nuanced science without human review.
- Revisit AI tools post-updates to gauge improvements.
