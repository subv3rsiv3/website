### Senator Slams Microsoft for Default Windows Kerberoasting Risks Senator Ron Wyden is taking Microsoft to task over how its default Windows settings, particularly in Active Directory, leave systems exposed to Kerberoasting attacks, as demonstrated in the Ascension ransomware breach. Hackers infiltrated via a contractor's laptop through Microsoft Edge and Bing, then exploited weak RC4 encryption to crack admin passwords and spread malware across the network. This outdated tech makes even supposedly strong passwords vulnerable to rapid GPU-based cracking, turning a minor breach into a major headache. For SMBs and MSPs, this underscores the dangers of sticking with factory defaults, potentially leading to costly downtime, data loss, and compliance issues that small teams can't afford. Worse yet, Microsoft's vague plans to fix this—announced quietly with no clear timeline—mean businesses are left to fend for themselves against evolving threats. Takeaways: - Beef up admin passwords to 14+ characters immediately.

        **Key takeaways**
        - Ditch RC4 encryption to thwart offline cracking risks.
- Audit Active Directory regularly for hidden vulnerabilities.
- Budget for potential breach costs like recovery and downtime.
