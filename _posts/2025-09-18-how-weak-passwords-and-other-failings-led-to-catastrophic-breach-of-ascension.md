### Ascension's Breach: A Wake-Up Call for Shoddy Security Basics Ascension, a major healthcare player, suffered a massive network breach thanks to weak passwords and outdated security practices, allowing attackers to exploit vulnerabilities like Kerberoasting. This incident highlights a failure to layer defenses—think security in depth—and adopt zero trust models, which assume breaches are inevitable and build in containment from the start. Instead of a fortified perimeter, Ascension's setup was more like a house with one lock on the front door, letting intruders roam freely once inside. For SMBs and MSPs, this underscores the real-world risks of skimping on cybersecurity, as even a single compromised device could cripple operations, lead to data loss, and invite hefty regulatory fines. Ultimately, it's a stark reminder that in 2025, relying on decade-old flaws isn't just lazy—it's a direct path to disaster that small businesses can't afford. Takeaways: - Layer defenses to limit breach spread, not just block entry.

        **Key takeaways**
        - Ditch weak passwords; enforce strong encryption now.
- Zero trust setup prevents total network takedowns.
- Regularly audit for old vulnerabilities like Kerberoasting.
- Budget for security tools to avoid costly downtime hits.
