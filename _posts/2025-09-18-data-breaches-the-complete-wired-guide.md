### Data Breaches: A Subvertec Rundown – Don't Get Hacked Like These Suckers Look, data breaches have been a tech headache since the '80s, starting with the 1984 TRW fiasco where a simple password leak exposed 90 million Americans' credit info via a prehistoric bulletin board—proof that lazy security has always been a bad idea. Fast-forward to the 2010s, and things got messier with breaches like LinkedIn's 2012 hack, which spilled 117 million user credentials (thanks to shoddy password habits), turning into a dark web goldmine that still bites users today. Retail giants weren't spared either: Target lost 40 million credit cards in 2013, Neiman Marcus followed suit with 1.1 million, and Home Depot coughed up 56 million more in 2014, all thanks to sneaky malware on payment systems. Then there's the 2013-2015 OPM breach by Chinese hackers, stealing Social Security numbers and fingerprints from 21.5 million folks—essentially handing over the keys to the US government's personnel vault. For SMBs and MSPs, this history screams a wake-up call: beef up your defenses with multi-factor auth, regular audits, and employee training, or risk becoming the next statistic in this endless game of digital whack-a-mole. **Key Takeaways for SMBs and MSPs:** - **Password hygiene isn't optional:** Stop reusing passwords across accounts—it's like leaving your front door wide open; use password managers and enforce strong policies to avoid multi-breach fallout.
