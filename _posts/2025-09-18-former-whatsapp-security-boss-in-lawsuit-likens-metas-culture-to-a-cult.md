### Former WhatsApp Security Chief Sues Meta Over Toxic Culture and Data Mess A former WhatsApp security head is taking Meta to court, blasting the company's rigid, unquestioning culture as cult-like and accusing it of ignoring critical data security flaws. This includes engineers snooping on user data, no proper inventory of user info as required by laws in California, the EU, and FTC agreements, and a lack of systems to track data storage or spot breaches—issues that are basic table stakes for any serious tech outfit. The lawsuit claims Meta retaliated against him for raising alarms, including failing to address daily hacks affecting hundreds of thousands of users and widespread data scraping that exposed profiles to scams. For SMBs and MSPs leaning on platforms like WhatsApp for communication and customer data, this mess underscores the risks of depending on big tech giants with sloppy governance, potentially exposing your business to breaches, legal headaches, and reputational damage that could hit your bottom line hard. It's a wake-up call to prioritize your own security setups, as relying on external tools without scrutiny might leave you vulnerable to the same pitfalls. Ultimately, this case shows how whistleblower protections and transparent practices aren't just nice-to-haves—they're essential for maintaining trust and compliance in a data-driven world. Takeaways: - Audit your data access controls to avoid internal leaks.

        **Key takeaways**
        - Build whistleblower policies to catch security issues early.
- Diversify communication tools beyond risky platforms like WhatsApp.
- Invest in basic monitoring to comply with privacy laws.
- Prepare for retaliation risks when reporting vulnerabilities.
