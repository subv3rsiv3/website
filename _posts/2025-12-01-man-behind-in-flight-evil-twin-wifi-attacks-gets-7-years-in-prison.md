A 44-year-old Aussie hacker just got slapped with a seven-year-plus prison sentence for pulling off sneaky "evil twin" WiFi attacks, where he used a WiFi Pineapple device to mimic legitimate airport and in-flight networks in places like Perth, Melbourne, and Adelaide, tricking travelers into handing over their social media creds via a phishing page. This creep then exploited those logins to spy on women's accounts, swiping intimate photos and videos—authorities uncovered thousands of such files during their raid, along with evidence of his frantic attempts to delete data and even hack his employer's laptop. While these attacks aren't everyday threats, they're a stark reminder that public WiFi can be a goldmine for bad actors, especially if you're not using a VPN or keeping an eye out for suspicious login prompts. For SMBs and MSPs, this mess underscores the need to train your teams on secure habits like enabling strong passwords, avoiding auto-connect features, and treating free WiFi like a potential trap—because one wrong click could expose your whole operation to similar digital shenanigans.
