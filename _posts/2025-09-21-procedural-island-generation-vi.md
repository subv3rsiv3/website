In the finale of CartoKit's procedural island series, we're wrapping up by baking your terrain into a sleek, reusable mesh packed with elevation, moisture, biome, and river data—think of it as turning raw math into a handy asset without the usual hassle of regenerating everything from scratch. This baked terrain feeds into a CPU-based debug renderer that spits out diagnostic images via simple rasterizers, ensuring your visualizations are consistent across platforms and perfect for quick tweaks or exports, which is a godsend for SMBs dodging pricey GPU setups. The interactive viewer, built with egui, lets you fiddle with parameters like rainfall or elevation hints in real-time, then export to GLB, PNG, or even GIFs with just a few lines of code, making it dead simple to integrate into your projects without reinventing the wheel. Performance is snappy—under 100ms for most ops—so small-business owners and makers can iterate fast, and with modular architecture, it's easy to swap parts for custom needs, paving the way for future add-ons like GPU shading or game engine exports.
