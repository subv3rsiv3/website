### Mis-issued TLS Certificates Threaten Cloudflare's DNS and Beyond Cloudflare's popular 1.1.1.1 DNS service recently had bogus TLS certificates issued, potentially allowing attackers to spoof the service and launch man-in-the-middle attacks that intercept user traffic. This slip-up highlights the fragile nature of the public key infrastructure (PKI), where a single certificate authority's mistake can undermine trust across the entire web. For SMBs and MSPs, who depend on reliable DNS for everything from email to e-commerce, this incident underscores how easily cybercriminals could eavesdrop on or alter sensitive data, disrupting operations and exposing vulnerabilities. It's a stark reminder that even tech giants like Cloudflare and Microsoft aren't immune to these blunders—Microsoft, in particular, dragged its feet in detecting the issue. Ultimately, this event shows why smaller businesses need to bolster their own security layers, as relying solely on big providers can leave you hanging when the digital castle crumbles. Takeaways: - Heightened risk of DNS hijacks exposing SMB customer data.

        **Key takeaways**
        - Potential costs from downtime during attack responses.
- Regularly audit certificate transparency logs for threats.
- Implement extra verification for critical DNS services.
