### Mis-issued TLS Certificates Threaten Cloudflare's DNS and Beyond Cloudflare's popular 1.1.1.1 DNS service recently had its TLS certificates mistakenly issued to an unknown party, potentially allowing attackers to spoof the domain and launch man-in-the-middle attacks that intercept and tamper with user traffic. This blunder highlights the fragility of the public key infrastructure (PKI), where a single certificate authority slip can undermine trust across the entire internet. For SMBs and MSPs, who depend on reliable DNS for secure operations like email and online transactions, this incident underscores how easily their data could be exposed or disrupted by similar oversights. It's a stark reminder that even top-tier services aren't foolproof, pushing small businesses to scrutinize their own security setups to avoid becoming collateral damage. Meanwhile, tools like Certificate Transparency logs, designed to catch these issues early, didn't flag this one for months, revealing gaps in oversight that could affect everyday online activities. Takeaways: - Risks of undetected certificates include data interception for SMB networks.

        **Key takeaways**
        - Boost certificate monitoring to catch mis-issues before attacks hit.
- Costs could spike with downtime from compromised DNS services.
- Actions: Audit your CA dependencies for potential weak links.
