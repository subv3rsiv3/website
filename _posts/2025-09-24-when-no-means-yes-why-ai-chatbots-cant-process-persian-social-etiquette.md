![When “no” means “yes”: Why AI chatbots can’t process Persian social etiquette](/assets/images/when-no-means-yes-why-ai-chatbots-cant-process-persian-social-etiquette-hero.webp)
Ever imagine your AI chatbot botching a simple transaction in Iran because it takes "no" at face value? That's exactly what's happening with popular language models like GPT-4o and Claude 3.5, which flunk the nuances of taarof—a Persian ritual of polite refusals and insistences—with accuracy rates hovering around a dismal 34-42%, compared to native speakers who nail it 82% of the time. A new benchmark called TAAROFBENCH, developed by researchers including Nikta Gohari Sadr from Brock University, puts these AIs to the test and exposes their Western-biased directness, potentially leading to awkward fails in global business chats. For SMBs and MSPs relying on AI for customer service or negotiations, this cultural blind spot could sour deals or offend partners, so it's high time to tweak your bots with some region-specific training. Meanwhile, if you're expanding into Persian markets, remember that a little human oversight goes a long way to avoid turning a courteous offer into a comedic catastrophe.
