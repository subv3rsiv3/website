![When “no” means “yes”: Why AI chatbots can’t process Persian social etiquette](/assets/images/when-no-means-yes-why-ai-chatbots-cant-process-persian-social-etiquette-hero.webp)
AI chatbots might dominate Western chit-chat, but they're fumbling hard with taarof, the Persian custom of polite refusals and insistences that turns a simple "no" into a ritualistic yes—think insisting on paying a taxi driver multiple times before they actually take your cash. A recent study from researchers at Brock and Emory universities introduced TAAROFBENCH, the first tool to test how LLMs like GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Haiku, and Llama 3 handle these cultural nuances, revealing they only nail taarof scenarios 34 to 42 percent of the time, compared to 82 percent for native speakers—even a Persian-finetuned model like Dorna flopped. This cultural blind spot means your AI-powered customer service or global comms tools could botch negotiations or alienate Persian-speaking clients, potentially costing SMBs and MSPs in international markets. If you're running a small biz with AI assistants, it's worth tweaking prompts or seeking culturally aware alternatives to avoid these awkward misfires and keep relationships smooth. Ultimately, this highlights how even the smartest AIs still need a crash course in global etiquette to truly go worldwide without stepping on toes.
