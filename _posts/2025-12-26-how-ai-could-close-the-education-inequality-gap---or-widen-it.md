AI's rise in education is a double-edged sword, potentially bridging the yawning inequality gap by offering affordable, always-on tutoring tools like custom chatbots that adapt to students' needs, much like how SMBs use AI to streamline operations without breaking the bank. Take Franklin School's savvy integration of AI for deeper learning and teacher efficiency, contrasting sharply with knee-jerk bans like NYC's on ChatGPT, which missed out on its potential to handle routine tasks and foster critical thinking. Experts like Ethan Mollick argue that AI already outperforms no-tutoring scenarios, especially for underserved kids, but warn it could widen disparities if only tech-savvy elites master it first—think of it as the broadband divide on steroids. For tech-curious pros and small-business owners, this means exploring AI edtech like Khan Academy's tools not just for your own upskilling, but as a way to equip your teams or clients with accessible resources that boost productivity without replacing human touch.
